I am a PhD candidate in economics at CREST and Télécom Paris (Institut Polytechnique de Paris), with an expected defence in May 2027. I work in applied microeconomics, with a focus on telework, consumer mobility and local consumption.

My research relies on very granular data made available through a partnership between Télécom Paris, Insee and Cartes Bancaires (CB). The thesis has two aims: research of high academic standard, and helping Insee extend its use of CB card data in official statistics. I am attached to Insee's Data Science Innovation Unit, where I work with a member of the team on the coverage and comparability of this source, which depends in particular on CB's market share.

Research interests: applied econometrics, industrial organisation, urban economics, spatial economics, consumer mobility and retail externalities.

Advisors: David Bounie (Télécom Paris) and John Galbraith (McGill University).

Research

Job market paper

The Consumer City Revisited: Consumption Responses to Real-Time Population Presence

with David Bounie, John Galbraith and Gabrielle Gambuli

Working Paper, March 2026. Submitted to Review of Economics and Statistics.

Abstract

This paper combines high-frequency mobile phone location data with card transaction records for the Lyon metropolitan area to estimate the elasticity of transactions with respect to the number of people present in each of over one thousand zones (Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood). The elasticity varies across time and space, with a peak on Saturday midday and lower values in peripheries than in urban cores. Spatial frictions reduce transaction flows with distance from home, and essential goods such as food retail respond more than discretionary sectors.

Working papers

The Impact of Telework on Local Consumption: Evidence from Mobile Phone and Transaction Data

with Gabrielle Gambuli, David Bounie and Etienne Come

Insee Working Paper (Documents de travail) No. 2026-16, September 2026. Submitted to Journal of the European Economic Association.

Abstract

Using mobile phone and card transaction data for the Lyon metropolitan area, the paper identifies two demand shocks from telework: a 1 percentage point rise in teleworking presence raises local spending by about 1%, while a 1 percentage point drop in on-site presence lowers it by about 1.3%. The net effect on weekday in-store spending is small and not significant; spending shifts toward residential suburbs.

Work in progress

Closure Externalities and Trip Chains

Single-authored

Abstract

The paper uses twelve months of card transaction data (May 2024 to April 2025) from the Centre-Val de Loire region to test whether the complementarities revealed by trip chains have economic content: when a store closes, do consumers who used to combine it with another store in the same outing keep visiting the surviving store? Chains are observed directly at the level of the card and the establishment, whereas the literature typically calibrates them on surveys or infers them from aggregates. An event study compares cards that chained the surviving store with the closing store to cards that chained it with a comparable store that stayed open. Visits to the surviving store decline after the closure.

Do Anchor Stores Generate Spillovers?

with Marie-Laure Allain

Abstract

The paper studies the June 2022 closure of a large cultural retail store in Paris using weekly card transaction data (CB) for establishments within one kilometre, matched to the Insee SIRENE register. Establishments closer to the closed store are compared with those closer to another store of the same chain that stayed open, in difference-in-differences and triple-difference designs. Competing cultural retailers nearby gain transactions after the closure; effects on complementary sectors (food and beverage, other retail, services) are smaller.

Shop Externality Revealed through the Consumer Journey

with Laura Gaimard

Insee Analyses (four pages), forthcoming April 2027.

Abstract

Institutional companion note to "Closure Externalities and Trip Chains", based on its descriptive results and extended to metropolitan France with CB card transaction data: share of chained transactions and spending, most frequent pairs of store types, and differences between urban and rural areas.
